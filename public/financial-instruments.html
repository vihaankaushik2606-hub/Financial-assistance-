Financial instruments.

Explore how different instruments work, what drives their value and which risks to investigate.

8 instruments

Equity

Stocks

A share represents ownership in a company. Returns can come from price changes and dividends; dividends are not assured.

What to examine
Company earnings, valuation, debt, governance and industry outlook.
Key risks
Prices can fall sharply, and shareholders can lose their investment.
Examples
Equity shares listed on NSE or BSE.

Debt

Bonds & debentures

Debt securities represent borrowing by an issuer. Terms specify repayment and any interest payments.

What to examine
Issuer credit quality, maturity, yield, duration and repayment terms.
Key risks
Default, interest-rate changes, inflation and difficulty selling.
Examples
Government securities and corporate bonds.

Debt

Money-market instruments

Short-term debt instruments help issuers manage near-term funding needs.

What to examine
Issuer, maturity, liquidity and yield after costs.
Key risks
Short maturity does not eliminate default or liquidity risk.
Examples
Treasury bills, commercial paper and certificates of deposit.

Funds

Mutual funds

Investors hold units in a pooled portfolio managed under a stated investment objective.

What to examine
Portfolio holdings, benchmark, expense ratio, concentration and exit terms.
Key risks
Risk depends on the underlying assets; diversification does not prevent losses.
Examples
Equity, debt and hybrid mutual fund schemes.

Funds

Exchange-traded funds (ETFs)

Fund units trade on an exchange. Many ETFs track an index; others follow different strategies.

What to examine
Underlying exposure, fees, tracking difference and trading spread.
Key risks
Market risk, tracking differences and premiums or discounts to asset value.
Examples
Broad-market equity ETFs, debt ETFs and gold ETFs.

Real assets

REITs & InvITs

Listed trust units provide exposure to real-estate or infrastructure assets through a pooled structure.

What to examine
Asset quality, occupancy or usage, debt, cash flows and distribution policy.
Key risks
Interest rates, asset concentration and changing cash distributions.
Examples
Listed real estate investment trusts and infrastructure investment trusts.

Derivatives

Futures & options

Contracts derive value from an underlying asset or benchmark. Futures create obligations; option buyers acquire rights under contract terms.

What to examine
Contract size, expiry, settlement, margin, volatility and payoff.
Key risks
Leverage magnifies losses. Option buyers can lose their premium; some written options can create unlimited losses.
Examples
Stock and index futures and options.

Deposits

Bank deposits

A bank deposit is a claim on a bank. Fixed deposits generally specify a term and interest rate.

What to examine
Interest terms, withdrawal rules, bank creditworthiness and applicable deposit protection.
Key risks
Inflation, early-withdrawal penalties and bank exposure.
Examples
Savings accounts and fixed deposits.
